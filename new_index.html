Count the Passes

Watch the white circle only. Count every pass it makes. No pausing, no rewinds — this works when you commit.

Track ONLY the WHITE circle STANDBY

How many passes did the white team make? Hold your number in your head — and ask yourself what else was on the screen.

Stare at the beak-or-ears. Can you flip it on purpose? Most people can — but not instantly. That delay is the finding.

The Impossible Triangle

Trace the three corners with your eyes. Each corner is a perfect right angle. The triangle cannot exist. Your brain built it anyway.

Penrose triangle · Constructed from real bars Forced depth interpretation

There is no place to put this object in the world. Geometry says impossible — perception says "clearly a triangle." Who overruled whom?

Experiment 05 · Live Now

The Memory Inception

Four words, two seconds each. Just look — no memorising tricks. Then the room votes on a word that never appeared.

Deese–Roediger–McDermott paradigm

Press RUN. Four words flash on this card — watch them like a slideshow, nothing else.

—

Which of these four words was on the screen just now?

Shout your answer before I reveal anything. The room will agree — that agreement is the data.

No notes, no repetition. Encoding happens whether you intend it or not.

Ref: Roediger & McDermott (1995) · False-memory rate ≈ 80% on this list

Experiment 07 · Live Now

Prove the Rule

The rule: every card with a vowel on its underside has an even number on the other. Four cards lie here. Turn over the minimum set that could break the rule — and watch how fast the room agrees on the wrong ones.

Wason selection task · your picks, live vowel → even

Tap the cards you would flip. Only those. Not the ones that would confirm the rule — the ones that could FALSIFY it.

Ref: Wason (1966) · ~10% pass rate in the abstract version

Field Manual · Part 2 · Closing

Three Defenses

You cannot turn the machinery off. You can, however, install three habits that catch it in the act.

01

Seek Disconfirming Evidence

Before you decide, ask out loud: "what would have to be true for me to be wrong?" Then go look for exactly that. Flip the 7, not the 4.

02

Beware Leading Questions

Hear the verb before you answer the question. "Smashed" versus "hit" is a difference of eight miles per hour — and a difference of fabricated memories.

03

Slow Down When It Feels Obvious

Conviction is a feeling, not a fact. When an answer arrives instantly and the room nods in unison — that speed is the alarm, not the reassurance.

Ethical constraint: every demonstration in this deck was designed to reveal the mechanism — never to leave anyone fooled. Trick responsibly: reveal immediately, explain honestly, and hand the audience the exit.
